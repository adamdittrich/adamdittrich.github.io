CZECHOSLOVAKIA'S EFFECTIVE EXCHANGE RATES, 1920–1938
Provenance and numerical reconstruction
Adam Dittrich · Documentation release 13 November 2026

1. RELEASE IDENTITY AND CONSTRUCTION

The Excel reader edition dated 22 October 2026 selects and reshapes the final
processed series in the author's publication archive:
Statistika_NEER_REER_Czechoslovakia_1920_1938 = PUBLICATION(4).zip.

The archive's file
02_Replication/data_processed/effective_exchange_rate_indices_1920_1938.csv
contains exactly the 455 available index observations represented in the
website's eer-all-series.csv: 247 nominal and 208 real observations, arranged
as thirteen specification pairs. All 455 values agree exactly when read as
numbers. The archive's bilateral panel has fourteen partners and nineteen
years, or 266 partner-year rows.

This documentation publishes the unchanged bilateral panel as
bilateral-inputs.csv, and the unchanged conversion ledger as
currency-unit-rules.csv. Their original filenames, paths and SHA-256 hashes
are recorded in source-manifest.json. The companion reproduce_indices.py
rebuilds the indices from raw quotations, conversion factors, prices and
trade values in that panel using only the Python standard library. Its
largest relative difference from the reader indices is approximately
7.22 × 10^-15, attributable to floating-point arithmetic. The full verification
counts are recorded in validation-report.json.

The related replication deposit is doi:10.3886/E251023V1:
https://doi.org/10.3886/E251023V1
The DOI identifies the related deposit cited by the publication package.
It is not a new identifier for the October reader edition or this November
documentation and analytical extension.

2. CURRENCY QUOTATIONS AND CONVERSION RULES

The input quotation is Czechoslovak crowns per reported foreign-currency
unit. The publication code multiplies that quotation by its conversion
factor before taking logarithms:

  e(i,t) = raw_quotation(i,t) × conversion_factor(i,t).

It does not invert the quotation or use a US-dollar intermediary at this
stage. For partner-years outside the rules below the factor is one.

Partner              Years with multiplier in the released code   Factor
Germany              1920–1924                                  1,000,000,000,000
Austria              1920–1924                                         10,000
Poland               1920–1923                                      1,800,000
Hungary              1920–1926                                         12,500
Belgium-Luxembourg   1920–1926                                              5

The successor units are respectively Rentenmark/Reichsmark, schilling,
zloty, pengő, and belga. The through_year field is the last annual input
observation to which the released implementation applies the multiplier;
it is not a legal currency-reform date. The original source citations and
URLs travel with currency-unit-rules.csv. Additional primary references are
listed in source-ledger.csv. These references support conversion ratios;
they do not, by themselves, establish which unit each original annual
statistical table used.

All 266 processed harmonised quotations were checked against the product
of the recorded raw quotation and applicable factor. This verifies the
released transformation, not each underlying historical transcription.

3. PRICE INDICES AND ANNUAL AGGREGATION

The bilateral real-rate logarithm is constructed as

  ln q(i,t) = ln e(i,t) + ln P(i,t) − ln P(CS,t).

Both price series are stored with 1929 = 100. Price inputs are complete for
the fourteen partners and Czechoslovakia from 1923 through 1938. The real
indices are unavailable before 1923; those cells remain blank.

The publication describes the observations as annual averages. The Python
construction reads annual cells directly from the input workbook's
data_long sheet. It does not construct those annual values from monthly or
daily records. The exact within-year averaging convention, treatment of
non-trading days, and any averaging of reciprocals are not encoded in this
package. The website therefore treats the outputs as annual observations
and does not infer the timing of a within-year currency movement.

The raw workbook's cpi_1923_1938 sheet records provenance labels. For most
partners and Czechoslovakia, 1923–1936 is labelled "old workbook Tabelle1"
and 1937–1938 "updated CPI data". Sweden and Belgium-Luxembourg are labelled
"updated CPI data" throughout 1923–1938. The exact country, year span and
worksheet rows are preserved in cpi-source-ledger.csv. These labels
identify the supplied input series; they do not specify original printed
page references or a splice formula. No splice or interpolation is added
by the website extension.

The publication cites official Czechoslovak foreign-trade volumes and
National Bank reports with associated historical series for trade,
exchange rates and domestic prices. Its files do not provide a complete
observation-by-observation concordance to original publication pages.
The source ledger distinguishes such general attributions from numerical
records that can be checked directly.

4. INDEX FORMULAS

For current-year, lagged-year, Törnqvist and fixed-1929 geometric chains,
the annual log link is the weighted sum of bilateral log changes. The
four methods use, respectively, current-year shares, previous-year shares,
the average of those two shares, or constant 1929 shares. Total-trade,
export and import weight families are normalised across the same fourteen
included partners. Chained levels are rebased to 1929 = 100.

The direct-current specification, supplied for total trade only, is

  I(t) = 100 × exp{Σ_i w(i,t) [ln z(i,t) − ln z(i,1929)]},

where z is e for the nominal index and q for the real index. This is a
current-weighted index of bilateral relatives to 1929. Its annual movement
also incorporates changes in weights on those relative levels.

For nominal and real chains with identical weights, the ratio
100 × REER(t)/NEER(t) is a cumulative chained relative-price component.
Its annual log change equals the weighted foreign inflation change less
the Czechoslovak inflation change under the same link weights. The
nominal–real difference for the direct-current formula also contains a
reweighting component and is not labelled an inflation-only contribution.

5. THE EARLY EXTENSION: A NUMERICAL TRACE

The released sample design is retained: 1920–1924 is the pre-stabilisation
extension and 1925–1938 is the analytical core. REER is technically
available from 1923. Early-year-links.csv exposes the inputs and
partner contributions to the preferred total-trade links ending in
1921–1925. Each contribution is 100 × the current-year weight × the
bilateral log change; the unit is log points, not an ordinary percentage
change. Real contributions are blank until both price observations exist.

For the link from 1923 to 1924, the preferred total-trade nominal change is
−684.5398 log points and the real change is −1,488.3786 log points. Germany
contributes −647.4406 nominal log points and −822.0900 relative-price log
points, for −1,469.5306 real log points. The remaining thirteen partners
together contribute −18.8480 real log points.

The German records contain a raw quotation of 0.0007058 crowns per
reported unit in 1923 and 0.000000000008067 in 1924. Multiplication by
10^12 yields harmonised quotations of 705,800,000 and 8.067. The supplied
German CPI is 1,032,716,043,376.0475 in 1923 and 85.04273504273503 in 1924;
the Czechoslovak CPI is 93 in both years. Those inputs account
arithmetically for the large early link and hence the early index levels.

This identifies the numerical origin of the magnitudes. It does not
independently establish the historical correctness or comparable unit
basis of every early quotation and price observation. The released data
are preserved. Core-period analysis and full-period logarithmic display
are separate views of the same observations.

6. PARTNER LABELS AND GEOGRAPHIC SCOPE

The fourteen labels are Austria, Belgium-Luxembourg, France, Germany,
Hungary, Italy, Netherlands, Poland, Romania, Sweden, Switzerland, United
Kingdom, United States, and Yugoslavia. The source workbook's README,
row 6, describes its United Kingdom, France and Netherlands trade entries
as including colonies. This is a source-workbook label; no partner-year
country/colony concordance is encoded. Belgium-Luxembourg is a combined
trade partner and is assigned the Belgian currency rule in the released
implementation. These conventions are recorded rather than inferred
from modern country names.

7. INTERPRETATION AND REUSE

Higher NEER means nominal depreciation; higher REER means real
depreciation. CPI-based REER is a broad relative-price indicator. Consumer
prices are only indirectly related to exporters' production costs and can
also reflect indirect taxes and subsidies. The 1929 reference level is a
normalisation; it does not estimate equilibrium value or misalignment.
For the general interpretation of CPI-based competitiveness indicators,
see the ECB's methodological caveats:
https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/balance_of_payments_and_external/hci/html/index.en.html
