Teaching fields

Areas I can teach

European economic history

Industrialisation, globalisation, war, depression, reconstruction, and changing forms of state intervention from the nineteenth century onward.

Monetary and financial history

Gold standards, central banks, banking crises, exchange-rate regimes, inflation, deflation, and the political economy of credibility.

History of economic thought

Competing traditions of value, causality, equilibrium, teleology, and economic order, with particular attention to Central Europe.

Cliometrics and historical data

Turning archival categories into analysable evidence; interpreting regressions historically; communicating uncertainty and sensitivity.

Approach

Three habits I want students to acquire

01
Ask what the variable meant historically. A modern label can conceal a different institution, accounting rule, or population.
02
Separate chronology from explanation. Knowing when a series turned is the beginning of analysis, not its conclusion.
03
Treat policy as a constrained choice. Historical actors faced legal, political, intellectual, and informational limits that a textbook model may omit.

Supervision and discussion

Student research

I am especially interested in student projects that combine a clearly bounded historical problem with careful source work: central-bank policy, banking and financial institutions, international trade, interwar Czechoslovakia, historical statistics, or Czech economic thought.

A good project need not begin with sophisticated econometrics. It should begin with a question for which the available evidence can distinguish among competing explanations.
